Layout draft · leave-one-out solver analysis

One row per solver, expandable to chains

Window: 2026-07-01 → 2026-08-01, four chains, 44 solvers, each removed from the auctions it entered on each chain it bid on. This page is a layout draft: the figures are real, the surrounding prose that a published artifact carries is not written here.

Why solver over chain. Removing one solver from mainnet and from base are independent counterfactuals, so summing a solver's rows across chains is a real scenario — that solver absent everywhere. Summing different solvers' rows on one chain is not a scenario at all: they are mutually exclusive removals. So chains nest under solvers, and a chain is a filter rather than a level of the hierarchy.

The window, one row per solver

Click a solver to see its chains. Selecting a chain flattens the table to that chain's solvers, re-sorted by that chain's Δsurplus — the “who matters here” view. Two figures cannot be aggregated and show — on a solver row: native amounts, because ETH + xDAI is not a quantity, and the median moved order, because a median of medians is not a median.

Column definitions are unchanged from the published format. On an aggregate row the USD figures and the order counts are sums; the coverage and win shares are ratios of those sums; Δprice is the USD-converted moved Δsurplus over the USD-converted volume, so each chain's own ratio stays exact and the blend across chains is weighted at each chain's median rate; the largest-auction share is the biggest single auction anywhere in the solver's rows over its USD total.
