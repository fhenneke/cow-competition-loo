Column totals are sums over 26 independent single-solver scenarios, not the effect of removing all 26 at once. With several solvers gone the remainder would replace one another, which is a different and much larger question.

What the columns measure

  • Δsurplus — the change in user surplus over every order either side's winners traded, so an order that stops trading contributes its whole surplus rather than dropping out of the comparison. The relative figure beneath spreads the moved-order surplus across the window's entire executed fill-or-kill volume, which is the average traded unit's price change; unmoved orders and orders that stop trading count as zero there, since their loss is the coverage column instead.
  • Δfees, gross — the whole protocol fee the mechanism charges, partner share included. This is what the DB calls protocol_fee_native.
  • of which — the same delta split into what the DAO keeps and what is passed on to partners. The split is positional, not by policy kind: the trailing policies in application order are the partners'. Partner fees are the larger half of the fee level but a much smaller share of its movement, because they are volume policies and so track whether an order fills at all rather than the price it gets.
  • Orders no longer executed — user-order executions that trade with the solver and not without it, against the 103,176 executions the month's recorded winners performed. Counted per auction-order execution, so a partially fillable order weighs as often as it trades.
  • Won — the recorded competition's own count, with the share of analysed auctions the solver bid in beneath it.
  • Δrewards, capped — the payout-scale reward change, with each reward clamped into the recorded caps. A replacement winner inherits the cap of the slot it displaces.

How the competition is reproduced

The counterfactual is a full re-arbitration, not merely a re-pick of winners: removing a solver can lower a per-token-pair baseline, which un-filters solutions that were unfair only because the removed solver set that baseline high.

The fairness filter compares solutions per token pair, and that split of a solution's score is recorded nowhere. It is recomputed from the protocol's own fee policies and anchored on each solution's recorded score. Where a pair's fees are unrecorded its user surplus stands in as a lower bound, so a missing fee can only make the filter stricter than the protocol was, never looser.

Replaying this month against the recorded competition reproduces it exactly: winners, filtered-out set and reference scores all 84,767 of 84,767, with 0 of 38,404 multi-pair filter decisions differing and 0 of 946,995 solutions whose recomputed fees contradict the recorded score. Across the 26 removals, 217 of 539,972 filter verdicts rested on a lower-bound guess, and in 76 auctions such a guess could have changed who wins — against 1,507 auctions where a removal genuinely un-filtered a solution.

Caveats — the numbers don't travel without these

  1. No behavioural response. The remaining solvers' bids are held fixed, so nothing here says how they would bid if a competitor actually left. The cap of three solutions per solver is applied before winners are picked, so a rival solution suppressed by that cap cannot step in either.
  2. Settlement risk is assumed, not observed. These figures use the inherited rule: a replacement winner takes the recorded outcome of the token pairs it claims, so a batch that really reverted stays reverted whoever wins it. The everything-lands-in-time reading was not run for this month, and the choice is first-order — on the calibration window 14.9% of winners never settled in time and they carried 50.2% of winning score.
  3. Rewards are an estimate at payout scale. A replacement winner inherits the reward cap of the slot it displaces. The uncapped figures are the mechanism's exact accounting but are not what is paid, so they are not shown.
  4. 1,146 auctions are excluded for a native price that fails a two-sided sanity check, and 720 more because a traded order appears in neither order table. Both exclusions apply identically to every solver.
  5. Fee deltas drop an auction from both sides when an executed order's fee policies are unrecorded — 445 auction-scenarios across the run, 0.06%. Surplus, coverage and reward columns are unaffected.
  6. Dollar figures are display conversions. Each auction converts at the rate implied by its own stablecoin prices, and they inherit every caveat of the ETH figure they restate.
  7. Relative price change over small samples is not stable. A solver with few moved orders can show a large figure drawn from a handful of auctions.
  8. Quote rewards are excluded. There is no data on counterfactual quoting.
