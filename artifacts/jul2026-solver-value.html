Leave-one-out solver analysis · CoW Protocol

Rizzolver, Baseline, and ExtQuasimodo without themselves: July 2026

Every auction of 2026-07-01 → 2026-08-01 re-arbitrated with one solver removed, on every chain that solver bid on: Rizzolver on 4 chains, Baseline on 10, ExtQuasimodo on 9 (it has no mainnet presence) — 23 solver-chain combinations. Headline Δsurplus figures use the inherited settlement scenario, explained with the table below.

What was measured: each auction’s winner selection is re-run on the recorded bids with one solver’s bids removed, and the outcome is compared with what actually happened. Signs: every delta is counterfactual − actual — negative Δsurplus means users would have received less without the solver, negative Δfees means the protocol would have collected less, and positive Δrewards means it would have paid more. USD figures are display-only conversions at each auction’s own stablecoin-implied rate, on every chain.

Headline findings

  • Rizzolver is the largest by every measure: ≈ $236k/month of user surplus and ≈ $132k of protocol fee. Averaged over everything each chain traded in July, its removal makes prices 0.63 bps worse on mainnet (≈ $2.1B of volume), 2.1 bps on bnb and 1.1 bps on base. 1.6% of mainnet’s executed orders and 0.5% of bnb’s and base’s traded only because it bid.
  • Baseline: ≈ $3.9k/month of surplus and ≈ $365 of fee, with its footprint in coverage on the small chains — 14.2% of everything xdai executed, 14.0% on plasma, 9.0% on avalanche and 5.2% on polygon traded only because Baseline was there. In dollars those orders are small (xdai’s 7,111 carry $6.73); in service terms they are the chain’s long tail.
  • ExtQuasimodo: ≈ $957/month of surplus and ≈ $519 of fee, three quarters of it on Gnosis ($714 surplus, $449 fee; 3.3% of the chain’s executions traded only through it). Everywhere else its removal moves single- to double-digit dollars.
  • Fee relative to surplus differs by solver. Rizzolver returns 56% of its surplus figure as protocol fee and ExtQuasimodo 54%, against 9% for Baseline, whose value sits in coverage of orders that carry little fee.
  • Chain size dwarfs win share. Baseline’s 41% win share on polygon is worth ≈ $1k/month; Rizzolver’s 10% share on mainnet is worth $184k.

“Δprotocol and partner fees” is the whole fee the winning solutions charged, the partners’ share included, with the auctions whose fees could not be priced noted beneath; “Δprotocol fee” is what the protocol keeps, with the partners’ share beside it. Both are summed over every order either side traded, so an order that stops trading contributes its whole fee. “Won” is the solver’s share of analysed auctions, with its bid rate beneath. “Δprice, all traded volume” spreads the moved-order Δsurplus over the window’s whole executed fill-or-kill volume; its sub-line is the conditional reading — the change on just the still-traded orders whose price moved, with the median moved order and the moved count. “Orders no longer executed” divides the orders only the solver executed by every user-order execution the chain’s recorded winners traded that month (partially fillable included; an order counts once per auction it executes in — see caveats), with the executions and the surplus they carried beneath. “Δrewards (capped)” is the payout-scale estimate. “Δsurplus (assume-settled)” is the everything-lands-in-time scenario. “Largest auction share” is the whale check — ⚠ marks headlines that are mostly one trade. Price-suspect auctions (0–1.6% per chain) and auctions with unrecoverable JIT order data (up to 2.3% on arbitrum) are excluded from every figure and enumerated in the report files.

What the fee columns measure

The competition ranks solutions on score, which is user surplus plus protocol fees, so a solver’s value has a fee component as well as a surplus one. The fee columns are recomputed from each auction’s recorded fee policies and reproduce the protocol’s own recorded score exactly on the solutions where both are available.

“Protocol fee” is used here in the budget sense: what the protocol keeps. The analytics database and the protocol’s own code use the same words for the gross amount including the partners’ share, so the two figures are labelled separately — Δprotocol and partner fees for the gross, Δprotocol fee for the remainder. On mainnet the partners take the larger half of the gross.

The partners’ share of the change ranges from about 0% on xdai and plasma to 74% on polygon. Partner fees are charged on volume, so they are unaffected when a rival wins the same order at a worse price and are lost only when the order does not trade; their share of the change therefore reflects how much of a solver’s value is coverage rather than price.

Reading each solver

Rizzolver

July’s number one on all four of its chains, on surplus and on fee alike. The mainnet figure (−$184k surplus, −$118k protocol fee) is broad-based: thousands of auctions move, the largest is 4% of the total, and the price deterioration is as wide as the volume — 2.5 bps on the ≈ $530M that moved, 0.63 bps averaged over everything. 17% of its surplus total is coverage (orders nobody else executed); the other 83% is the sturdier claim — orders a rival would still have filled, at a worse price. Its fee is priced the same way, and the partners’ share of it is 12%. Quote arbitrum carefully: 31% of its −$5.3k is one auction.

Baseline

Modest in dollars everywhere despite huge win shares on the long-tail chains — those chains simply carry little surplus, and on the crowded chains the next bidder reproduces its trades almost exactly. 64% of its surplus is coverage, and that is where the service story lives: one in seven of xdai’s and plasma’s executed orders would simply not have traded. Those orders carry little fee, which is why its fee figure (≈ $365) is 9% of its surplus figure rather than the ~55% the other two return.

ExtQuasimodo

Material only on Gnosis, where it accounts for $714 of surplus and $449 of protocol fee. On plasma it wins 35% of auctions with a negative own net reward — competitive pressure whose removal would raise rivals’ payouts — but at plasma’s scale that is ≈ $15/month.

Caveats — the numbers don’t travel without these

  1. No behavioural response. Rivals’ bids are held fixed; nobody re-quotes or re-routes because a competitor vanished. This matters most exactly where the findings are largest — a 41% win share disappearing would surely attract entry, and nothing here models that. The competition’s cap on solutions per solver is also applied before winners are picked, so a rival solution suppressed by that cap cannot step in either.
  2. Settlement scenario. Winning an auction does not guarantee the batch lands on-chain, so the counterfactual must assume something about settlement — the two Δsurplus columns above are the two readings. The gap between them is genuine uncertainty about whether reverted batches would have landed — widest on polygon ($1,013 vs $1,506).
  3. USD is display-only. Converted per auction from the auction’s own stablecoin native prices; nothing on the valuation or reward path consumes a USD number. Native amounts remain the exact figures.
  4. Two reward figures exist. The table shows the capped estimate (the payout-scale answer, with replacements inheriting the displaced slot’s cap). Uncapped accounting figures, and Δsurplus net of capped Δrewards, are in the full comparison output.
  5. Exclusions are named, not silent. Price-suspect auctions and missing-JIT-data auctions are excluded from every statistic and enumerated in the report files; cap-orphan auctions drop only from the capped aggregate.
  6. Quote rewards are out of scope — no data on counterfactual quoting.
  7. Whale-shaped rows are flagged. ExtQuasimodo on avalanche (71%) and polygon (100%), Rizzolver on arbitrum (31%), Baseline on ink (100%): those headlines are one or few auctions; per-auction inspection is part of reading any row.
  8. Fees are exact where they are priced, and the exceptions are counted. Protocol fees are recomputed from the recorded fee policies and reproduce the protocol’s own score exactly. A fee cannot be computed when a solution the real competition filtered out wins the counterfactual, because policies are only recorded for solutions that were kept; those auctions drop from both sides of the fee comparison and are counted in the table (0–59 per row, under 0.3% everywhere). Nothing is read as a fee of zero.
  9. “Protocol fee” here excludes the partners’ share. The database and the protocol’s own code use “protocol fee” for the gross amount; this page uses it for what the protocol keeps, and labels the gross figure “protocol and partner fees”. Reading the gross number as protocol revenue overstates it — by about a factor of two on mainnet, and by more on polygon, where partners take 91% of the fee level.
  10. Fee figures are gross revenue. The protocol also pays rewards, which often move the other way; the two are reported side by side and not netted, since the fee side is exact while the capped reward side is an estimate.
  11. “Orders no longer executed” is a share of everything the chain executed. The column divides the orders only the solver executed by every user-order execution the chain’s recorded winners traded that month, partially fillable orders included; an order counts once per auction it executes in, so a partially fillable order weighs as often as it trades. The narrower per-auction reading — relative only to auctions the solver bid in — lives in the report files; it overstates chain-level dependence for a selective bidder and is not shown here.
  12. Δprice comes in two conditionings, labelled. The headline figure answers “when a price moves, by how much” — over fill-or-kill user orders trading on both sides whose surplus moved; the “overall” figure spreads the same Δsurplus over all executed fill-or-kill volume, counting unmoved orders as zero price change. The two differ by up to two orders of magnitude and neither substitutes for the other. Rows with few moved orders (linea’s 96 bps is 9 orders) are anecdotes, not run rates; the moved-order count is printed with every figure.

Method: full re-arbitration per auction (fairness filter included) on recorded scores; executed amounts from proposed executions; “executed” means settled in time. Protocol fees recomputed from the recorded fee policies, validated against the recorded score and against the accounting model’s own partner/protocol split. Reports: 46 files, format 6 — regenerated 2026-08-29 with the fee columns; every figure carried over from the 2026-08-17 run reproduces it exactly. Produced by loo analyse / loo compare.
